This document outlines a program development plan and LLM (Large Language Model) development prompts for an application designed to enhance reading ability, drawing extensively from the provided sources and the conversation history. The app aims to guide users through structured stages to improve reading speed, comprehension, and flexibility by addressing common challenges like subvocalization and inefficient eye movements.

## Program Development Plan: "ReadWise"

### 1. Vision and Mission

**Vision:** To empower individuals to become highly efficient and flexible readers, capable of optimizing their reading approach for any text or purpose, thereby unlocking new levels of learning and comprehension.

**Mission:** To provide a science-backed, personalized, and engaging training platform that systematically guides users through a multi-stage process to manage subvocalization, improve visual processing, and deepen comprehension, transforming reading from a passive activity into an active, enjoyable, and powerful tool for growth.

### 2. Target Audience

The app targets a broad audience, including:
*   **Students** (high school, college, graduate level) who need to process large volumes of academic texts efficiently.
*   **Professionals** who read extensively for work (e.g., developers, researchers, lawyers) to enhance their problem-solving and knowledge acquisition.
*   **General readers** seeking to improve their reading speed and comprehension for personal development, enjoyment, and lifelong learning.
*   Individuals struggling with **inefficient reading habits**, particularly those who feel limited by subvocalization or slow reading speeds.

### 3. Core Principles and Philosophy

The "ReadWise" app will be built upon the following principles:

*   **Science-Backed Approach:** All techniques and explanations will be directly supported by cognitive science and reading research, dispelling common myths about speed reading.
*   **Balanced Speed and Comprehension:** The primary goal is not just speed, but enhancing reading efficiency without compromising comprehension. It acknowledges the **"trade-off"** between speed and understanding, especially for complex texts.
*   **Reading Flexibility (Reading Gears):** Users will learn to adapt their reading speed and strategy based on the text type and their purpose (e.g., skimming for quick information vs. deep reading for complex concepts).
*   **Subvocalization Management, Not Elimination:** The app recognizes that inner speech (mukdok) is a natural and often essential part of comprehension, distinguishing it from pathological subvocalization. The focus will be on optimizing its use and reducing its dependency when not needed, rather than complete eradication.
*   **Active Learning and Practice:** Consistent, focused practice and active engagement with the text are crucial for habit transformation and cognitive enhancement.

### 4. Key Features by Stage/Module

The app will offer a structured, progressive learning path, with each module building upon the previous one.

#### Module 0: Onboarding & Initial Assessment
*   **WPM (Words Per Minute) Test:** A standardized test to establish the user's baseline reading speed and comprehension. Users will read a passage for a set time (e.g., 1 minute) and then answer comprehension questions.
*   **Subvocalization Self-Diagnosis Checklist:** A series of questions and simple tests (e.g., counting aloud while reading) to help users identify their subvocalization habits (e.g., lip/tongue movement, strong inner voice).
*   **Reading Habits Questionnaire:** Questions about current reading challenges, goals, and preferred genres to personalize the learning path.
*   **Personalized Initial Report:** Provides a summary of baseline performance and a suggested starting point within the training modules.

#### Module 1: Foundational Speed & Subvocalization Control
*   **Lessons:**
    *   **Understanding Subvocalization:** Clear explanation of subvocalization, distinguishing between pathological subvocalization (physical muscle movement), silent reading/inner speech (cognitive "hearing"), and auditory imagination. Emphasize that mukdok is normal, but inefficient mukdok is a problem.
    *   **The Phonological Bottleneck:** Explain how the speed of inner speech can limit reading speed (around 150-250 WPM, rarely exceeding 300 WPM for most people).
    *   **Importance of Visual Processing:** Introduce the concept of reading directly from sight to meaning, bypassing unnecessary sound conversion.
*   **Exercises:**
    *   **Physical Subvocalization Suppression:**
        *   **Chewing Gum/Candy:** Users are instructed to chew gum or hold candy in their mouth while reading to occupy speech muscles.
        *   **Humming/Counting Drill:** Users hum or repeat simple, single-syllable sounds (e.g., "one, two, three, four" or "da-da-da") aloud or mentally to interfere with the inner voice. This can allow for near-complete elimination of subvocalization but may initially reduce comprehension.
        *   **Finger Tapping:** Tapping fingers on a surface at a steady rhythm while reading, which can reduce subvocalization with less cognitive interference than counting.
    *   **Basic Pacer/Pointer Drill:** Guide users to smoothly follow lines of text with a finger or on-screen pointer at a consistent, slightly faster-than-comfortable pace to reduce regressions (re-reading parts) and encourage continuous forward movement.
    *   **Conscious Fast Reading Bursts:** Short, timed sessions (e.g., 1-3 minutes) where users intentionally try to read much faster than usual, even if comprehension initially drops, to break psychological barriers and train the brain to rely more on visual cues.
    *   **Eye Stretching/Warm-up Exercises:** Simple drills to improve eye movement efficiency, such as focusing on a finger moving side-to-side or tracing an infinite loop.

#### Module 2: Enhanced Visual Processing & Chunking
*   **Lessons:**
    *   **Chunking (Meaning Units):** Explain the concept of perceiving multiple words or phrases as single units of meaning, moving beyond word-by-word reading. This addresses the phonological bottleneck by reducing the number of "sound units" processed.
    *   **Perceptual Span:** Introduce the idea of widening the visual area perceived with each eye fixation.
*   **Exercises:**
    *   **Chunking Drills:** Progressive exercises starting with 2-3 word chunks, then expanding to larger meaning units (phrases, clauses, entire sentences, or even paragraphs). The app can use visual highlights (e.g., block highlighting or "Spritz-like" rapid serial visual presentation for short bursts as a training tool, with a clear warning about its impact on comprehension for actual reading).
    *   **Advanced Pacer Techniques:** Users practice jumping between focus points within a line (e.g., focusing on the beginning, middle, and end of a line) to capture more words per fixation.
    *   **Visualization Training:** Encourage users to "paint pictures" or "create videos" in their mind as they read, shifting cognitive processing from the phonological loop to the visuospatial sketchpad. This promotes deeper, intuitive, meaning-based understanding.
    *   **Background Music Integration:** Offer curated instrumental music (e.g., Baroque, Lo-fi) at low volumes to subtly occupy the auditory channel, reducing subvocalization intensity without direct linguistic interference.

#### Module 3: Strategic Reading & Comprehension Deepening
*   **Lessons:**
    *   **Reading Flexibility:** Emphasize that proficient readers adjust their speed and method according to the text's difficulty and their reading purpose.
    *   **Importance of Background Knowledge:** Discuss how prior knowledge (schema) enables faster comprehension and "predictive reading."
    *   **Active Reading Strategies:** Introduce techniques like questioning, summarizing, and note-taking as crucial for deep understanding and retention.
*   **Exercises:**
    *   **Adaptive Speed Training:** Users practice reading different text types (e.g., news articles, novels, academic papers) at optimal, flexible speeds, with guided exercises on when to skim and when to read deeply.
    *   **Active Questioning Prompts:** The app prompts users to ask questions before, during, and after reading to engage critical thinking and foster curiosity, helping them identify the author's intent or challenge arguments.
    *   **In-App Summarization Tools:** Provide guided exercises and templates for summarizing sections or entire texts in the user's own words, reinforcing the principle that understanding is deepened by outputting information.
    *   **Vocabulary & Background Builder:** Integrated features to identify unknown words, provide definitions, and suggest related content to build schema.
    *   **Environment Optimization Tips:** Provide advice on creating an optimal reading environment, such as minimizing digital distractions and ensuring proper lighting.

#### Module 4: Progress Tracking & Personalized Coaching
*   **Detailed Analytics:** Track WPM, comprehension scores, and accuracy for different text types over time. Visualize progress and identify trends.
*   **Adaptive Difficulty:** Content selection adjusts dynamically based on the user's performance, ensuring a continuous challenge without overwhelming them.
*   **Personalized Learning Path:** AI-powered recommendations for next exercises, specific text types, or areas to focus on, based on user data.
*   **AI Coach:** An integrated LLM-powered coach provides real-time feedback, encouragement, and answers user questions about reading strategies.
*   **Community Features (Future consideration):** Leaderboards, shared reading lists, discussion forums to foster motivation and shared learning.

### 5. Technology Stack (Brief)
*   **Platform:** Mobile (iOS, Android), potentially web application.
*   **Frontend:** React Native (for cross-platform compatibility), Swift/Kotlin for native performance if needed.
*   **Backend:** Cloud-based (AWS/Google Cloud/Azure) with scalable services (e.g., Node.js, Python/Django/Flask).
*   **Database:** PostgreSQL, MongoDB (for flexible content schema).
*   **AI/ML:** Integration of custom LLM APIs for content generation, feedback, and personalization.

### 6. Monetization Strategy (Example)
*   **Freemium Model:** Basic modules and a limited content library are free.
*   **Subscription Tiers:** Premium access to advanced modules, full content library, personalized AI coaching, detailed analytics, and new features.

## LLM Development Prompts for "ReadWise" App

These prompts are designed for an LLM to generate dynamic, personalized content and feedback within the "ReadWise" app. Each prompt specifies the input the LLM would receive from the app's system and the desired output format and content, drawing heavily from the scientific literature.

### 1. Explanatory Content Generation (for Lessons/Tooltips)

**Purpose:** To provide clear, concise, and scientifically accurate explanations of reading concepts and techniques.

**Prompt 1.1: Explaining Subvocalization (General)**
*   **Input:** `{"topic": "subvocalization", "audience_level": "beginner", "context": "introduction to speed reading"}`
*   **Output:** "Explain what subvocalization is, distinguishing between the common understanding (inner voice, silent reading) and the clinical definition (physical muscle movement). Explain why it's a 'habit' for many and its potential impact on reading speed. Cite."
*   **LLM Role:** Informative, clarifying.

**Prompt 1.2: Explaining the Phonological Loop**
*   **Input:** `{"topic": "phonological loop", "audience_level": "intermediate", "context": "cognitive processes in reading"}`
*   **Output:** "Describe Baddeley and Hitch's 'Phonological Loop' as a component of working memory. Explain its two parts (phonological store and articulatory control process) and how the latter relates to subvocalization, acting as a 'core engine' for comprehending sentences and retaining information in working memory. Use an analogy if helpful. Cite."
*   **LLM Role:** Educational, detailed explanation.

**Prompt 1.3: Explaining Reading Flexibility**
*   **Input:** `{"topic": "reading flexibility", "audience_level": "advanced", "context": "strategic reading"}`
*   **Output:** "Explain the concept of 'reading flexibility' for advanced readers. Detail why a proficient reader changes their reading speed and strategy based on text density (high-density vs. low-density) and purpose (deep comprehension vs. information retrieval/skimming). Provide examples of WPM ranges for different text types. Cite."
*   **LLM Role:** Strategic, nuanced.

Explain that this technique helps reduce subvocalization by creating a 'distraction' for the inner voice, potentially with less comprehension cost than counting. ### 3. Comprehension Check & Feedback

**Purpose:** To assess user comprehension and provide personalized, constructive feedback.

**Prompt 3.1: Summary Evaluation**
*   **Input:** `{"text_title": "[Title of Text]", "user_summary": "[User's Typed Summary]", "key_points_of_text": ["Point 1", "Point 2", "Point 3"], "target_comprehension_depth": "medium"}`
*   **Output:** "Evaluate the provided user summary for '[Text Title]' based on accuracy, completeness relative to the key points, and whether it's expressed in the user's own words. Provide specific feedback on strengths and areas for improvement. If the summary is too literal, advise rewriting it using their own language and thoughts to deepen understanding, as this active process is crucial for memory and application. If key points are missing, suggest re-reading those sections with an active questioning mindset. Cite."
*   **LLM Role:** Evaluative, coaching, encouraging active recall.

**Prompt 3.2: Comprehension Question Feedback (After RSVP Training)**
*   **Input:** `{"exercise_type": "RSVP_training", "user_answer_correctness": "low", "comprehension_issue": "regression_inhibition"}`
*   **Output:** "Provide feedback for low comprehension after RSVP training. Explain that while RSVP apps can train visual processing speed, they are known to significantly reduce comprehension, especially for complex texts, because they prevent 'regression' (re-reading difficult parts). Emphasize that regression is a necessary cognitive activity for understanding complex structures or concepts. Advise using RSVP only as a 'training tool' for speed, not for deep learning, and encourage integrating other comprehension-focused strategies. Cite."
*   **LLM Role:** Diagnostic, corrective, guiding.

**Purpose:** To suggest optimal next steps based on the user's progress and identified learning needs.
